## 3. How to Take Perfect Screenshots

Since LifeFlow is designed with a **Mobile-First** approach, it looks best in a vertical mobile aspect ratio!

* **Aspect Ratio:** 9:16 (Standard smartphone ratio).
* **How to Capture:** 
  1. Open the app in Chrome/Edge.
  2. Press `F12` to open Developer Tools.
  3. Click the **Device Toggle Toolbar** icon (or press `Ctrl + Shift + M`).
  4. Select **iPhone 12 Pro** or **Pixel 7** from the dropdown at the top.
  5. Refresh the page to ensure all mobile constraints load perfectly.
  6. Use your snipping tool to capture just the app screen.
* **Presentation (The "Wow" Factor):**
  Don't just post raw screenshots! Go to a free mockup site like **[Shots.so](https://shots.so/)** or use **Canva**. Upload your raw screenshots there, and it will place your app inside a beautiful iPhone frame with a colored, shadow-dropped background. This makes it look incredibly professional for LinkedIn and your README.

## 4. Screen Recording & Video Compression

Videos can take up a lot of data and slow down your GitHub README. Here is the best way to handle recordings:

### Recording the Video
* Keep your browser in the **Mobile Inspect Mode** (as mentioned above).
* Use a tool like OBS Studio, Loom, or just the built-in Windows Snipping Tool (which now has video recording!).
* Only record the exact bounds of the mobile screen, not your whole desktop. Keep it short (30-45 seconds max). Show the Kanban board, adding an expense, and the Dashboard.

### Uploading for LinkedIn
* **Format:** LinkedIn loves standard `.mp4` files.
* **Compression:** If the file is too large, use a free tool like [Handbrake](https://handbrake.fr/) or [FreeConvert](https://www.freeconvert.com/video-compressor) to compress the `.mp4`. Aim for under 20MB.

### Uploading for your GitHub README
* **Should you use a GIF?** YES! A GIF automatically plays on a GitHub README without the user needing to click play, which is highly engaging.
* **How to convert and compress:** 
  1. Go to [Ezgif.com (Video to GIF)](https://ezgif.com/video-to-gif).
  2. Upload your short MP4.
  3. Set the frame rate to `10` or `15` fps (keeps file size tiny).
  4. Once generated, use their **"Optimize"** tool to compress it further. Aim for a GIF that is **under 5MB**.
  5. Place the GIF directly in your `README.md` using standard image tags: `<img src="demo.gif" width="300">`. 
  6. *Alternative:* If you want high-quality video in your README, upload the `.mp4` to YouTube as an "Unlisted" video, and put a thumbnail in your README that links to the YouTube video!
